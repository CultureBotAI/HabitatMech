Coalbed methane well water anoxic coal-derived aromatics/acetate and trace-element cofactor regime, Methanobacterium/Methanosarcina/Acetobacterium/Pseudomonas guild selection, and mcrA-driven biogenic coalbed methanogenesis mechanism

Subsurface formation and produced waters from coalbed methane (CBM) wells constitute closed, anoxic, bicarbonate-buffered hydrological systems rich in coal-derived polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (such as naphthalene) and acetate but constrained by trace-element micronutrient (Co, Cu, Mo, Ni, Fe) availability. This subsurface coal-seam aqueous regime selects for syntrophic bacterial degraders and acetogens (Pseudomonas stutzeri, Acetobacterium, Geobacter, and sulfate-reducing bacteria) paired with hydrogenotrophic (Methanobacterium subterraneum, Methanobacterium formicicum) and acetoclastic (Methanosaeta, Methanosarcina) methanogenic archaea. In response to coal organics and trace-element enzyme cofactors, these communities transcribe methyl-coenzyme M reductase subunit alpha (mcrA) alongside bacterial naphthalene-degradation and nitrogen-fixation pathways to convert coal-derived aromatics, acetate, and H2/CO2 into biogenic coalbed methane.
