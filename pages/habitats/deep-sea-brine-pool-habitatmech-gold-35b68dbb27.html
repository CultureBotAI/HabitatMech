Causal graphs

Deep-sea brine pool hypersaline anoxic halocline gradient, Sulfurimonas/Sulfurovum, Candidatus Nitromaritima, and Methanohalophilus guild selection, and mcrA/dsrA/ectoine/rTCA biogeochemical cycling mechanism

Oceanic deep hypersaline anoxic basins (DHABs, or deep-sea brine pools) in the Red Sea and Eastern Mediterranean Sea feature sharp brine-seawater interfaces (haloclines) with extreme salinity (up to 155 PSU or 240 g L-1 NaCl), hydrostatic pressure (up to 35 MPa), elevated temperature (up to 52 degrees C in Atlantis II Deep), deoxygenation, and high dissolved sulfide. These physicochemical gradients select for stratified chemolithoautotrophic sulfide-oxidizing Campylobacterota (Sulfurimonas and Sulfurovum), halotolerant nitrite-oxidizing Candidatus Nitromaritima RS, Deltaproteobacteria sulfate-reducing bacteria, and piezophilic methylotrophic methanogens such as Methanohalophilus profundi. These guilds encode and express methyl-coenzyme M reductase (mcrA), dissimilatory sulfite reductase (dsrA), nitrate and nitrite reductases, reductive tricarboxylic acid (rTCA) cycle enzymes, and ectoine/hydroxyectoine biosynthesis enzymes to couple sulfide and nitrite oxidation with carbon fixation, methylotrophic methanogenesis, sulfate reduction, and compatible-solute osmoprotection across the brine-seawater interface.
