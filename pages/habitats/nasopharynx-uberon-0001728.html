Causal graphs

Nasopharyngeal mucosal interface and Corynebacterium gatekeeper colonization resistance mechanism

The upper respiratory nasopharyngeal mucosa forms an aerated, lipid- and mucin-coated epithelial interface colonized by commensal Corynebacterium species (C. accolens, C. pseudodiphtheriticum) that act as respiratory gatekeepers by competitively blocking epithelial adherence, hydrolyzing host surface triacylglycerols via LipS1 into bactericidal free fatty acids that inhibit Streptococcus pneumoniae, and secreting protease factors that block Staphylococcus aureus hemolytic toxins.
