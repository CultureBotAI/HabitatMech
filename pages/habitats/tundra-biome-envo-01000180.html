Tundra biome active-layer and transition-zone thaw iron-redox cycling and methanogenesis partitioning mechanism

In northern tundra soils, warming and extended thaw across the active layer and permafrost transition zone create suboxic, organic-carbon-rich reducing conditions modulated by soil pH and terminal electron acceptor (TEA) availability. Extended thaw enriches iron-cycling Gammaproteobacteria (Fe(III)-reducing Rhodoferax sp. and Fe(II)-oxidizing Gallionella sp.) by 3-5 orders of magnitude to 65% of community abundance and increases MtrE and Cyc1 cytochrome gene abundance coupled to benzoate and pyruvate/acetate oxidation, suppressing acetoclastic methanogenesis until Fe(III) TEAs are depleted or multi-year transition-zone warming expands Methanosarcinales.
