Anaerobic digester sludge saccharide/VFA loading, Syntrophomonas-Syntrophobacter-Methanothrix syntrophic assembly, and acetoclastic/CO2-reductive methanogenesis mechanism

Anaerobic digester sludge systems treating organic waste and wastewater sludge maintain anoxic, high-organic-loading environments where polymeric saccharides (such as starch) are hydrolyzed into volatile fatty acids (VFAs including butyrate, propionate, valerate, and acetate) under controlled solids retention times (SRTs) and conductive mineral (magnetite) regimes. Substrate-driven selection and VFA shock loads enrich for primary saccharolytic Bifidobacterium, syntrophic butyrate- and propionate-oxidizing bacteria (Syntrophomonas, Syntrophobacter, Cloacimonadaceae W5, Peptoclostridium), and acetoclastic/CO2-reducing methanogenic archaea (Methanothrix soehngenii / Methanosaeta and Methanosarcina). These consortia encode propionate and acetate oxidation enzymes, glycine cleavage systems, acetoclastic methanogenesis genes, and the complete CO2 reduction pathway in Methanothrix soehngenii, catalyzing syntrophic VFA degradation and resilient methane-rich biogas production.
