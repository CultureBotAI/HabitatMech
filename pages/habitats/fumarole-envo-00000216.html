Causal graphs

Fumarole and solfatara acidic geothermal CH4/C2-C3 alkane/H2S/S0 gas emissions, Methylacidiphilum fumariolicum and Acidianus/Sulfolobus guild selection, and XoxF/PMO3/ba3-oxidase/sulfur-oxidoreductase cycling mechanism

Volcanic fumaroles and solfataras vent hot, extremely acidic geothermal steam and gases enriched in methane (CH4), short-chain alkanes and their hydrothermal oxidation products (ethane, propane, 2-propanol, acetone), hydrogen sulfide (H2S), and elemental sulfur (S0). These thermoacidic, gas-rich conditions select for thermoacidophilic verrucomicrobial methanotrophs such as Methylacidiphilum fumariolicum SolV alongside sulfur-metabolizing thermoacidophilic Crenarchaeota of the genera Acidianus and Sulfolobus. Methylacidiphilum fumariolicum SolV expresses lanthanide-dependent XoxF methanol dehydrogenase and its physiological electron acceptor cytochrome cGJ (cyt cGJ), acetone monooxygenase (PMO3), sulfide-oxidizing enzymes, and a sulfide-insensitive ba3-type terminal oxidase, while Acidianus and Sulfolobus express sulfur-oxidizing enzymes coupled to respiratory heme-copper oxidases as well as membrane-bound hydrogenase and sulfur reductase to drive simultaneous CH4, C3-compound, H2S, and elemental sulfur oxidation in solfataric steam vents.
