Causal graphs

Root endosphere host-filtered interior, water-deficit, and nitrogen-limitation regime, Streptomyces/Pseudomonas/Burkholderia/Bradyrhizobium endophyte guild selection, and acdS/nifH/SOD/siderophore/T6SS ethylene modulation and drought-resilience mechanism

The root endosphere is a host-gated internal plant root compartment shaped by plant immune and physiological filtering, soil water deficit (drought stress), and nitrogen availability. Relative to the external rhizosphere, these internal root cues and abiotic stresses selectively enrich specialized root-endophytic Actinobacteria (especially Streptomyces), Gammaproteobacteria (Pseudomonas), Betaproteobacteria (Burkholderia-Caballeronia-Paraburkholderia), and diazotrophic Alphaproteobacteria (Bradyrhizobium, Devosia, Sphingomonas, Allorhizobium-Neorhizobium-Pararhizobium-Rhizobium). Within the root interior, these endophytic guilds encode and upregulate 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylate (ACC) deaminase (acdS), nitrogenase (nifH), denitrification enzymes (nirS, nirK), superoxide dismutase (SOD), chitinase, siderophore and spermidine/colanic acid biosynthesis clusters, and type VI secretion system (T6SS) genes to cleave the plant stress-ethylene precursor ACC, scavenge reactive oxygen species, fix and transform nitrogen, and promote host root and shoot biomass under drought and low-nitrogen stress.
