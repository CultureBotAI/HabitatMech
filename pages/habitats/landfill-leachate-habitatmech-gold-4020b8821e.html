Landfill leachate high free-ammonia, salinity, and Zn(II) heavy-metal regime, Candidatus Kuenenia/Candidatus Brocadia/n-DAMO/denitrifier guild selection, and hzsAB/hdh/amoA/nirKS/mcrA/czcABC nitrogen removal and metal-efflux mechanism

Mature and fresh landfill leachates are high-strength engineered wastewaters characterized by elevated ammoniacal nitrogen and free ammonia (FA), high salinity, recalcitrant organic carbon, nitrite/nitrate fluctuations, and toxic divalent heavy metals such as Zn(II) (50-150 mg/L). In leachate treatment bioreactors and biofilms, long-term free-ammonia, salinity, and heavy-metal pressures select for salt- and FA-tolerant anaerobic ammonium-oxidizing (anammox) Planctomycetes (Candidatus Kuenenia, including Candidatus Kuenenia stuttgartiensis, and Candidatus Brocadia), ammonia-oxidizing bacteria, heterotrophic denitrifiers (Proteobacteria and Bacteroidetes), and nitrate/nitrite-dependent anaerobic methane-oxidizing (n-DAMO) archaea and bacteria. These guilds express hydrazine synthase (hzsA, hzsB), hydrazine dehydrogenase (hdh), hydroxylamine oxidoreductase (hao), ammonia monooxygenase (amoA), nitrate and nitrite reductases (narG, napA, nirK, nirS), methyl-coenzyme M reductase (mcrA), and RND heavy-metal efflux pumps (czcA, czcB, czcC) to couple partial nitrification, anammox, heterotrophic denitrification, and n-DAMO with active Zn(II) export.
