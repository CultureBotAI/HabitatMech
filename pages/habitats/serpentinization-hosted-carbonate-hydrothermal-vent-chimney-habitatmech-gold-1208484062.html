serpentinization-hosted carbonate hydrothermal vent chimney

Causal graphs

Serpentinization-hosted carbonate chimney alkaline H2/CH4/formate fluid flux, Lost City and Prony Bay Methanosarcinales and Thermodesulfovibrionales guild selection, and mcrA/dsrB/acs/nif/fdh syntrophic cycling mechanism

Serpentinization of uplifted mantle peridotite generates warm, hyperalkaline (pH 9-11), dissolved-inorganic-carbon-depleted hydrothermal fluids highly enriched in molecular hydrogen (H2), methane (CH4), and formate that mix with cold seawater to precipitate porous carbonate hydrothermal vent chimneys such as those at the Lost City and Prony Bay hydrothermal fields. Flow of these H2- and CH4-rich fluids through interior chimney walls selects for dense archaeal biofilms dominated by serpentinite-specialist Methanosarcinales (Lost City and Prony Bay phylotypes) alongside sulfate-reducing Thermodesulfovibrionales (Nitrospirota), Desulfovibrionales/Desulfobacterales, Firmicutes, Chloroflexi, and Candidatus Bipolaricaulota. This syntrophic community encodes and expresses methyl coenzyme M reductase A (mcrA), dissimilatory sulfite reductase subunit B (dsrB), AMP-forming acetyl-CoA synthetase (acs), nitrogen fixation (nif) genes, hydrogenases, and formate dehydrogenase-like enzymes to couple H2-stimulated methane production and anaerobic methane oxidation with sulfate reduction, acetate cycling, and diazotrophy across the porous carbonate matrix.
