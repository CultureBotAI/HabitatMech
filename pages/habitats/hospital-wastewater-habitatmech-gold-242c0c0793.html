Causal graphs

Hospital wastewater sub-MIC carbapenem and chlorine disinfectant regime, carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales and sewer-biofilm guild selection, and blaNDM/blaKPC/blaVIM/blaOXA-48/intI1/OmpK35-36 resistance dissemination mechanism

Hospital wastewater and sewer-pipe effluents combine clinical pathogen loads with sub-inhibitory concentrations of beta-lactam and carbapenem antibiotics (such as imipenem) and chlorine-containing hospital disinfectants. This selective regime enriches multidrug-resistant and carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (Klebsiella pneumoniae including ST11, Escherichia coli, and Citrobacter freundii) as well as sewer-pipe biofilm colonizers (Aeromonas, Pseudomonas, Stenotrophomonas, and Acinetobacter). These populations carry and express acquired carbapenemase genes (blaNDM-1, blaKPC-2, blaVIM, blaOXA-48, blaGES) alongside class 1 integron integrase (intI1) on conjugative IncX3, IncFII, and 80-kb plasmids and ISKpn19 composite transposons, often combined with outer-membrane porin OmpK35 and OmpK36 loss, driving elevated carbapenem minimum inhibitory concentrations, disinfectant-tolerant biofilm persistence, and interspecies horizontal plasmid transfer.
