black smoker

ENVO:00000218 ·resolve ·AQUATIC ·EXACT REVIEWED

A hydrothermal vent found on the ocean floor. The vents are formed in fields hundreds of meters wide when supeRHEAted water from below the Earth's crust comes through the ocean floor. The supeRHEAted water is rich in dissolved minerals from the crust, most notably sulfides, which crystallize to create a chimney-like structure around each vent. When the supeRHEAted water in the vent comes in contact with the cold ocean water, many minerals are precipitated, creating the distinctive black color. The metal sulfides that are deposited can become massive sulfide ore deposits in time. — ENVO

Deep-sea black smoker hydrothermal vent chemolithoautotrophy and hyperthermophilic sulfate reduction mechanism

Deep-sea black smoker hydrothermal vents discharge superheated volcanic fluids rich in reduced hydrogen (H2), sulfide, sulfite/thiosulfate, and dissolved minerals that mix with cold ambient seawater across steep spatial and chemical gradients, selecting for focused-vent chemosynthetic Epsilonproteobacteria (Sulfurimonas, Caminibacter, Arcobacter) and Gammaproteobacteria (SUP05) in the vent effluent and plume while supporting hyperthermophilic Archaeoglobus archaea (50-85 degrees C) that couple H2 oxidation to sulfate/sulfite/thiosulfate reduction and CO2 fixation via the reductive acetyl-CoA pathway.
