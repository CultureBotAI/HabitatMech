Coral reef holobiont DMSP release, Endozoicomonas/Roseobacter DddD/DddL lyase cleavage, and Pseudovibrio tropodithietic acid pathogen defense mechanism

Reef-building scleractinian corals produce high concentrations of dimethylsulfoniopropionate (DMSP) across coral tissue, skeleton, and mucus, structuring coral-associated bacterial communities dominated by Endozoicomonas, Roseobacter, Pseudovibrio, Spongiobacter, and Vibrio. Coral-associated Endozoicomonas, Roseobacter, and skeletal microbiomes express DMSP cleavage and synthesis genes including dddD, dddL, and dsyB to convert DMSP into climate-active dimethyl sulfide (DMS) and acrylic acid for thermal stress mitigation, while coral-associated Pseudovibrio sp. P12 catabolizes DMSP via TDA biosynthetic homologs to produce the antimicrobial tropodithietic acid (TDA), inhibiting invasive coral pathogens Vibrio coralliilyticus and Vibrio owensii.
