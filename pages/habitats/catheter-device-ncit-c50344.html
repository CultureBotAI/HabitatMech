Causal graphs

Indwelling catheter polymer surface, deposited host fibrinogen, and urinary urea regime, Proteus mirabilis/Staphylococcus epidermidis/Enterococcus faecalis biofilm guild selection, and MrpH/urease/icaADBC/EbpA crystalline encrustation and biofilm mechanism

Indwelling medical catheters (such as urethral and intravascular catheters) present abiotic silicone/polymer luminal surfaces coated with host-released fibrinogen and flushed by urea- and ion-bearing urine as well as sub-inhibitory antibiotic or external stresses. These foreign-body surface conditions select for polymicrobial and monospecies catheter biofilm colonizers including urease-producing Proteus mirabilis, coagulase-negative Staphylococcus epidermidis, and multiantibiotic-resistant Enterococcus faecalis. On the catheter surface, Proteus mirabilis expresses mannose-resistant Proteus-like (MR/P) fimbriae with tip adhesin MrpH and cytoplasmic urease, Enterococcus faecalis expresses the endocarditis- and biofilm-associated pilus (Ebp) tip adhesin EbpA (binding host fibrinogen via its N-terminal domain EbpA(NTD)), and Staphylococcus epidermidis expresses the icaADBC operon (subject to IS256-mediated phase variation in icaC) synthesizing polysaccharide intercellular adhesin (PIA). Together these adhesins, polysaccharides, and urease drive fibrinogen- and polymer-dependent biofilm accumulation, urea hydrolysis that elevates urinary pH, and precipitation of calcium phosphate and magnesium ammonium phosphate (struvite/apatite) crystals that encrust and occlude the catheter lumen.
