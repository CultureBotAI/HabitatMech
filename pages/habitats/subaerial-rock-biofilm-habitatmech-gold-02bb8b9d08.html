Subaerial rock biofilm desiccation/UV stress, Nostoc CrtB-dependent PEP-CTERM/scytonemin trafficking, and lithic water retention mechanism

Subaerial rock biofilms (SABs) on exposed stone and monument surfaces experience fluctuating water activity governed by local temperature and relative humidity alongside intense ultraviolet (UVA/UVB) radiation and drought. Phototroph-chemotroph SAB consortia (including filamentous cyanobacteria such as Nostoc punctiforme) employ cyanoexosortase B (CrtB) to export PEP-CTERM proteins that stabilize extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) and enable UVA/UVB-inducible biosynthesis of the indole-alkaloid sunscreen pigment scytonemin, while desiccated SABs enrich the matrix with proteins and DNA, shift metabolism to fermentation, and delay water evaporation from the underlying stone.
