Causal graphs

Subgingival dental plaque anaerobic crevicular heme/iron and matrix-protein niche, Porphyromonas gingivalis and Treponema denticola guild selection, and RgpA-Kgp/RgpB/FimA/dentilisin coaggregation and tissue-destruction mechanism

Subgingival dental plaque in the periodontal pocket is an anaerobic biofilm habitat bathed in gingival crevicular fluid and blood-derived hemoglobin that imposes an obligate requirement for iron and protoporphyrin IX on proteolytic periodontal pathobionts. This subgingival microenvironment selects for the keystone periodontal pathogen Porphyromonas gingivalis (predominantly fimA genotype II paired with kgp biotypes 381, HG66, and W83) coaggregating with the spirochete Treponema denticola. Porphyromonas gingivalis expresses Arg- and Lys-specific cysteine proteinases encoded by rgpA, rgpB, and kgp that assemble into cell-surface RgpA-Kgp proteinase-adhesin complexes (containing adhesin domains A1 and A3) alongside major fimbriae (fimA), while Treponema denticola expresses the chymotrypsin-like surface proteinase dentilisin. FimA binding to dentilisin and RgpA-Kgp adhesin binding mediate interspecies subgingival plaque coaggregation and epithelial attachment, while Kgp and RgpA/B proteolysis hydrolyzes hemoglobin for heme assimilation, penetrates gingival connective tissue along a concentration gradient, dysregulates IL-8/IL-6/sICAM-1/MCP-1 cytokine signaling, and induces host epithelial and fibroblast apoptosis and alveolar bone resorption.
