soil crust

habitatmech:GOLD.b1b5044bde ·TERRESTRIAL ·UNGROUNDED REVIEWED

A soil consisting of a coherent surface crust layer at the uppermost millimeters of soil, formed either by biological soil crust (biocrust) communities of cyanobacteria, algae, lichens, bryophytes, and heterotrophic microbes or by physical raindrop-impact and mineral crusting processes. — HabitatMech

Causal graphs

Biological soil crust Microcoleus vaginatus EPS habitat filtering, desiccation-resuscitation transcriptional response, and uc/ah-mediated cyanosphere urea-nitrogen mutualism mechanism

Arid and polar biological soil crusts (biocrusts) experience recurrent desiccation-hydration cycles, high irradiance, and nitrogen limitation on unconsolidated soil surfaces, selecting for the pioneer filamentous cyanobacterium Microcoleus vaginatus (such as M. vaginatus PCC 9802 and CM01) and heterotrophic diazotrophs (such as Massilia sp. METH4) in its cyanosphere. During wetting and drying cycles, M. vaginatus coordinates DNA repair, photosynthesis recovery, oxidative/osmotic stress responses, and exopolysaccharide (EPS) synthesis, while overexpressing urea uptake and the high-affinity urea carboxylase/allophanate hydrolase (uc/ah) system in response to urea secreted by cyanosphere diazotrophs, driving mutualistic C-for-N exchange, deterministic bacterial network assembly, and arid topsoil stabilization.
