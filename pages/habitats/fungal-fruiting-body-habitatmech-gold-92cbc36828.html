Fungal fruiting body Bradyrhizobium nifH nitrogen-fixing symbiosis and Pseudomonas GacS-EDB anti-nematode defense mechanism

Fungal fruiting bodies (ascocarps of Tuber magnatum white truffles and basidiocarps of Agaricus bisporus mushrooms) provide specialized hyphal microhabitats that host coevolved bacterial communities while facing grazing pressure from fungivorous/bacteriovorous nematodes such as Caenorhabditis elegans. Within Tuber magnatum fruiting bodies, dominant Alpha-Proteobacteria (Bradyrhizobium, Rhizobium, Ensifer) carry nitrogenase (nifH) and glutamine synthetase II (glnII) genes and exhibit reciprocal growth dependency with fungal mycelium, while mushroom-associated Pseudomonas fluorescens (strain NZI7) uses the global regulator GacS and the GacS-regulated EDB gene cluster to repel C. elegans predation.
