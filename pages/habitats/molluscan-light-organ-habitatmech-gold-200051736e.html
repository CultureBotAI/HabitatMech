Causal graphs

Squid light organ V-type ATPase acidic mucus recruitment, Vibrio fischeri RscS-Syp biofilm aggregation, and LuxR-LuxI-LuxA bioluminescence morphogenesis mechanism

Juvenile Hawaiian bobtail squid (Euprymna scolopes) hatch aposymbiotically and recruit their specific bacterial symbiont Vibrio (Aliivibrio) fischeri from seawater across juvenile-specific ciliated epithelial fields whose V-type ATPase (VHA) proton pump creates an acidic mucus microenvironment containing low-pH-optima antimicrobials. Directed to surface quiet zones by ciliary movements, V. fischeri uses the sensor kinase RscS (rscS) to activate transcription of the symbiosis polysaccharide (syp) gene locus (including sypN) alongside cellulose polysaccharide and the LapV adhesin, forming a transient surface biofilm aggregate before dispersing into deep crypt spaces. Inside the crypts, V. fischeri induces the luminescence regulatory and enzymatic proteins LuxR, LuxI, and LuxA (luxA, luxI, luxR), catalyzing bioluminescence and oxygen consumption that induce swelling of host crypt epithelial cells and sustain full colonization density.
