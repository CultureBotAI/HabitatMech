Volcanic geothermal acidity, sulfide/gas geochemistry, and thermoacidophilic archaeal-bacterial colonization mechanism

Active volcanic edifices, solfataric fields, and acidic hot springs create high-temperature (60-85+ degrees C), low-pH (pH 0-6.4), sulfide- and proton-rich geochemical regimes that select for thermoacidophilic and hyperthermophilic archaea (Sulfolobus, Acidilobus, Thermoplasma, Picrophilus) and acidotolerant thermophilic bacteria (Thermoanaerobacter), driving proton-coupled membrane transport, autotrophic carbon fixation, and anaerobic organotrophic/lithotrophic metabolism.
