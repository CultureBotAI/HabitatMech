Solar saltern sediment thalassohaline hypersaline anoxic and heavy-metal gradient, sulfate-reducing bacteria and halophilic Euryarchaeota guild selection, and dsrAB/methylamine-methanogenesis/metal-ATPase benthic cycling mechanism

Benthic sediments underlying solar saltern evaporation and crystallizer ponds experience extreme thalassohaline sodium and chloride concentrations (up to 97-148 ppt Cl- and crystallizer saturation), anoxia, methylated amine accumulation, and localized heavy-metal enrichment. This hypersaline anoxic regime selects for a stratified benthic community combining diverse sulfate-reducing bacteria (including Desulfovermiculus and Deltaproteobacteria), sulfur-oxidizing Thioalkalispira, halophilic Halobacteriaceae (Halobacterium salinarum, Natronomonas, Haloarcula, Halorubrum), and methylotrophic methanogenic archaea (Methanosarcinaceae such as Candidatus Methanosalis and MBSL-1 candidate division euryarchaeota). These benthic guilds express dissimilatory (bi)sulfite reductase (dsrAB), trimethylamine and dimethylamine methylotrophic methanogenesis enzymes, potassium-uptake and compatible-solute synthesis/transport genes, and heavy-metal resistance transporters (copper-translocating P-type ATPases and cobalt-zinc-cadmium resistance proteins) to drive benthic sulfate reduction, methylotrophic methane emission, and osmotic and metal-stress homeostasis.
