Causal graphs

Atmospheric air UV and desiccation survival filtering and proteinaceous biological ice nucleation mechanism

Atmospheric air across the convective boundary layer, troposphere, and lower stratosphere (up to 38 km altitude) exposes aerosolized microorganisms to severe desiccation, subzero freezing, and shortwave ultraviolet radiation, filtering for ATP-viable bacteria and airborne microalgae (Trebouxiophyceae, Chlorophyceae, and Stramenopiles) that tolerate desiccation and freezing and produce cell-associated proteinaceous ice nucleation active (INA) compounds catalyzing ice formation at warm subzero temperatures (>= -6 degrees C) to promote cloud and precipitation deposition.
