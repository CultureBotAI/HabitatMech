Leaf-cutter ant fungus garden Leucoagaricus gongylophorus cellulase/laccase Lac1Lg and Enterobacteriaceae diazotrophic/glycosyl-hydrolase symbiosis mechanism

Leaf-cutter ants (Atta and Acromyrmex spp.) cultivate subterranean fungus gardens using fresh, nitrogen-poor foliar plant biomass colonized by the obligate basidiomycetous fungal cultivar Leucoagaricus gongylophorus and a conserved bacterial community dominated by Enterobacteriaceae (Enterobacter, Pantoea, Klebsiella, Citrobacter, and Escherichia). Within this external digestive matrix, L. gongylophorus produces stage-specific lignocellulases including cellulases and the monomeric yellow laccase Lac1Lg, while symbiotic Enterobacteriaceae and N2-fixing bacteria express nitrogenase, bacterial glycosyl hydrolases, and B-vitamin/amino acid biosynthetic proteins, catalyzing recalcitrant plant polymer deconstruction and biological N2 fixation that is incorporated into host ant biomass.
