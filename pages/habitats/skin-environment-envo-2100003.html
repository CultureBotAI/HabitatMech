Cutaneous sebaceous lipid remodeling, acid-mantle selection, and colonization resistance mechanism

The human and mammalian skin surface imposes a desiccated, acidic, and sebaceous triacylglycerol-rich barrier landscape that selects for lipophilic and halotolerant Actinobacteria (Cutibacterium acnes, Corynebacterium spp.) and Firmicutes (Staphylococcus epidermidis), which hydrolyze host sebaceous lipids into free fatty acids and short-chain fatty acids that reinforce cutaneous acidity, modulate host immunity, and inhibit opportunistic pathogens such as Streptococcus pneumoniae and Staphylococcus aureus.
