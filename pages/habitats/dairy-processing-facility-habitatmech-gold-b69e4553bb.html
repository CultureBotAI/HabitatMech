Dairy processing facility stainless-steel biofilm persistence and psychrotrophic Pseudomonas AprX metalloprotease milk spoilage mechanism

Dairy processing facilities combine refrigerated raw milk storage (4-6 degrees C), milk-fed stainless-steel processing surfaces, and ultra-high-temperature (UHT) thermal regimes that select for resilient environmental Pseudomonas oligotypes (Pseudomonas fluorescens and Pseudomonas fragi) alongside multispecies stainless-steel biofilm producers (Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Bacillus licheniformis, Enterococcus faecalis, Streptococcus thermophilus). At refrigeration temperatures, P. fluorescens expresses the aprX-lipA2 operon to secrete the Zn2+/Ca2+-stabilized thermostable alkaline metalloprotease AprX, which survives UHT treatment and hydrolyzes casein micelles, causing milk destabilization and gelation.
