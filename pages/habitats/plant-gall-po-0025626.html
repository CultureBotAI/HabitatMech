Causal graphs

Plant gall host-wound signal and vascular parenchymal niche, Agrobacterium tumefaciens and Pantoea agglomerans gall-inducing guild selection, and Ti-plasmid VirD2/VirE2/iaaM/ipt/6b and pPATH T3SS phytohormone- and opine-driven gall morphogenesis mechanism

Plant galls (such as crown galls and bacterial stem/root galls) develop at wounded plant surfaces where host phenolic and sugar wound signals and vascular parenchyma cells interface with plasmid-bearing phytobacteria. These plant wound signals are recognized by two-component regulatory systems that select for and activate tumorigenic Rhizobiaceae (Agrobacterium tumefaciens / Agrobacterium radiobacter carrying the tumor-inducing Ti plasmid) and gall-forming Erwiniaceae (Pantoea agglomerans pv. gypsophilae and pv. betae carrying the pPATH pathogenicity plasmid). Agrobacterium expresses the Ti-plasmid vir regulon, delivering single-stranded T-DNA escorted by endonuclease VirD2 and ssDNA-binding protein VirE2 through a type IV secretion system (T4SS) into the host nucleus (via VirD2-importin alpha and VirE2-nucleoporin CG1 interactions) to integrate and express the oncogenes iaaM, ipt, and 6b alongside opine synthases, while Pantoea agglomerans expresses pPATH-encoded type III secretion system (T3SS) effectors, ShcV/CesT chaperones, and auxin/cytokinin biosynthetic enzymes, driving localized auxin and cytokinin overproduction, opine synthesis, and hyperplastic plant gall tumor development.
