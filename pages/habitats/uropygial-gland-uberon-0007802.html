uropygial gland

UBERON:0007802 ·resolve ·HOST_ASSOCIATED ·EXACT REVIEWED

A large gland at the base of a bird's tail dorsal to the cloaca at the end of the pygostyle that secretes an oil used in preening. It opens through a caudally directed nipple. — UBERON

Causal graphs

Avian uropygial gland lipid secretion, Enterococcus faecalis mutualistic symbiont selection, and enterocin MR10A/MR10B and AS-48 bacteriocin-mediated feather and eggshell protection mechanism

The avian uropygial (preen) gland is a holocrine secretory organ at the base of the tail that produces hydrophobic fatty secretions, which in nesting females and nestlings of the hoopoe (Upupa epops) harbor dense mutualistic bacterial communities dominated by Enterococcus faecalis (including strain MRR 10-3). Uropygial-gland Enterococcus faecalis strains encode and secrete the broad-spectrum bacteriocins enterocin MR10A (5201.58 Da) and MR10B (5207.7 Da) — homologous to enterocins L50A and L50B — together with enterocin AS-48. When preened onto plumage or smeared into specialized eggshell crypts, these bacteriocin-producing enterococci inhibit keratinolytic Bacillus licheniformis feather degradation and reduce trans-shell pathogen colonization to increase hatching success.
