Endolithic rock pore far-red light and UV/desiccation regime, Chroococcidiopsis, Halothece, and Chloroflexota guild selection, and FaRLiP/scyB/uptake-hydrogenase survival mechanism

Pore spaces and fissures within translucent desert rocks and evaporitic halite or gypsum crusts in hyperarid deserts (such as the Atacama Desert and Antarctic McMurdo Dry Valleys) buffer thermal and moisture extremes while filtering incident solar radiation, creating a microhabitat depleted in visible light, enriched in far-red light (FRL), and exposed to residual UV-A irradiation and prolonged desiccation. These lithic conditions select for endolithic primary-producer cyanobacteria of the genera Chroococcidiopsis and Halothece alongside heterotrophic and trace-gas-oxidizing Chloroflexota bacteria. Endolithic cyanobacteria express the 15-gene Far-Red-Light Photoacclimation (FaRLiP) cluster including chlorophyll f synthase and phycobilisome core-membrane linker apcE2 together with the scytonemin biosynthesis gene scyB, while co-occurring Chloroflexota encode high-affinity uptake hydrogenases and carbon monoxide oxidation enzymes to sustain FRL oxygenic photosynthesis, extracellular UV-screening scytonemin accumulation, and trace-gas-driven atmospheric chemosynthesis and metabolic water production.
