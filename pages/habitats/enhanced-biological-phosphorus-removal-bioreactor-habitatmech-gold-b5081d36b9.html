EBPR bioreactor alternating anaerobic feed/aerobic famine cycling, Candidatus Accumulibacter phosphatis and Defluviicoccus selection, and PPK1/PPK2/PhoU-Pst polyphosphate accumulation mechanism

Enhanced biological phosphorus removal (EBPR) wastewater treatment bioreactors subject activated sludge biomass to alternating anaerobic carbon-feed and aerobic famine regimes. This cyclic redox and substrate regime selects for polyphosphate-accumulating organisms (PAOs) of the lineage Candidatus Accumulibacter phosphatis (spanning at least five ppk1-defined clades) alongside competing glycogen-accumulating organisms (GAOs) of the genus Defluviicoccus (Ca. Defluviicoccus tetraformis, D. vanus, Ca. D. seviourii). Candidatus Accumulibacter expresses polyphosphate kinase 1 (ppk1) and a laterally acquired polyphosphate kinase 2 (PPK2) coupled with a dysregulated PhoU signaling protein that leaves the high-affinity phosphate transport (Pst) system constitutively active, condensing continuous inorganic phosphate uptake into intracellular polyphosphate reserves during aerobic famine while Defluviicoccus synthesizes glycogen and anaerobic polyhydroxyalkanoate (PHA).
