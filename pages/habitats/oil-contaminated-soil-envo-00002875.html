Causal graphs

Oil-contaminated soil biosurfactant emulsification and Rhodococcus/Actinobacteria alkB-todC1-pcaG hydrocarbon biodegradation mechanism

Crude-oil- and petroleum-hydrocarbon-contaminated soils impose hydrophobic organic solvent toxicity and low aqueous hydrocarbon bioavailability, selecting for solvent-tolerant Actinobacteria (notably Rhodococcus erythropolis) and Proteobacteria. These degraders produce glycolipid biosurfactants that lower surface tension to 38 mN/m to emulsify and mobilize crude oil while expressing alkane monooxygenases (alkB, alkB1), aromatic ring-hydroxylating dioxygenases (todC1, nidA, pcaG), RND efflux transporters (HAE1), and trehalose-6-phosphate synthase (otsA1) to catabolize aliphatic and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons.
