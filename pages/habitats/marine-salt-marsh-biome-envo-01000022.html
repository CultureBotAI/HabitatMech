marine salt marsh biome

ENVO:01000022 ·resolve ·AQUATIC ·EXACT REVIEWED

The marine salt marsh biome comprises marshes that are transitional intertidals between land and salty or brackish marine water (e.g.: sloughs, bays, estuaries). It is dominated by halophytic (salt tolerant) herbaceous plants. The daily tidal surges bring in nutrients, which tend to settle in roots of the plants within the salt marsh. The natural chemical activity of salty (or brackish) water and the tendency of algae to bloom in the shallow unshaded water also allow for great biodiversity. — ENVO

Marine salt marsh Spartina redox-coupled sulfate reduction, sulfide oxidation, and C/N fixation mechanism

Marine salt marshes dominated by smooth cordgrass (Spartina alterniflora) generate steep sediment redox potential gradients and high rates of enzymatic organic matter decomposition in the vegetated root zone, selecting for sulfate-reducing bacteria, chemolithotrophic sulfur-oxidizing diazotrophs (Candidatus Thiodiazotropha), and anoxygenic phototrophic purple/green sulfur bacteria (Chromatiaceae and Chlorobiaceae) that detoxify sulfide in the root zone while coupling sulfur oxidation to carbon and nitrogen fixation.
