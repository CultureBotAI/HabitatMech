Marine hadopelagic zone extreme hydrostatic pressure and sinking-particle alkane/recalcitrant-DOM regime, Alcanivorax/Oleibacter/Marinimicrobia/Chloroflexi/Thaumarchaeota guild selection, and alkB/almA alkane hydroxylation and deep-trench biogeochemical cycling mechanism

The marine hadopelagic zone in ocean trenches at depths below 6,000 m to ~11,000 m (such as the Mariana Trench Challenger Deep) is characterized by low temperature, extreme hydrostatic pressure, recalcitrant dissolved organic matter (DOM), and periodic fluxes of n-alkanes released from subduction-zone serpentinization and sinking particles. These extreme pelagic trench conditions select for distinctive hadal planktonic communities dominated by heterotrophic Marinimicrobia, recalcitrant-DOM-degrading Chloroflexi, chemolithoautotrophic Thaumarchaeota, Nitrospirae, and Rhodospirillales, and a dense bloom of aliphatic hydrocarbon-degrading Gammaproteobacteria (Alcanivorax including Alcanivorax jadensis, Oleibacter, and Thalassolituus) at depths >10,400 m. These hadopelagic guilds encode and transcribe alkane monooxygenase (alkB) and flavin-binding monooxygenase (almA) genes alongside extracellular peptidases, amino acid metabolism and DNA mismatch-repair systems, and nitrification/sulfur-oxidation pathways to drive piezotolerant n-alkane oxidation, recalcitrant organic matter mineralization, and sequential nitrification in the deepest ocean waters.
