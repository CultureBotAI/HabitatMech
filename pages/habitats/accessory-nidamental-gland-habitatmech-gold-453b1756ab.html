accessory nidamental gland

habitatmech:GOLD.453b1756ab ·HOST_ASSOCIATED ·UNGROUNDED REVIEWED

A reproductive gland in the mantle cavity of female decapodiform cephalopods that develops epithelium-lined tubules supporting an extracellular bacterial consortium. Developing glands can precede sexual maturity and dense colonization; mature glands can supply bacteria to egg jelly. — HabitatMech

Causal graphs

Cephalopod accessory nidamental gland tubule recruitment, Leisingera indigoidine/secondary metabolite biosynthesis, and egg clutch antifungal defense mechanism

Female bobtail squids (Euprymna scolopes) and other cephalopod mollusks develop a specialized reproductive organ, the accessory nidamental gland (ANG), composed of morphologically heterogeneous ciliated and nonciliated epithelium-lined tubules whose morphogenesis requires exposure to environmental bacteria (including Verrucomicrobia). The spatially partitioned ANG tubules house dense bacterial assemblages dominated by Rhodobacterales (Roseobacter clade genera Phaeobacter and Leisingera sp. JC1), Rhizobiales, Verrucomicrobia, and Flavobacteria. ANG symbionts such as Leisingera sp. JC1 encode the complete biosynthetic gene cluster for the antimicrobial pigment indigoidine alongside siderophore, acyl-homoserine lactone quorum-sensing, and antifungal secondary metabolite pathways (producing lincomycin, mycinamicin-like compounds, and glycerophosphocholines), which are deposited into the egg jelly coat to inhibit marine vibrios and pathogenic fungal biofouling by Fusarium keratoplasticum.
