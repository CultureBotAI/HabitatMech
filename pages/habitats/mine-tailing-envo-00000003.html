Causal graphs

Mine tailing sulfide mineral oxidation gradient, Acidithiobacillus/Leptospirillum/Ferroplasma succession, and Cyc2/rusticyanin extracellular iron oxidation and acid mine drainage mechanism

Metal-sulfide mine tailings undergoing oxidative weathering generate progressive acidification gradients from primary and slightly acidic tailings to extremely acidic, heavy-metal-rich oxidized tailings. This acidification succession selects for chemolithoautotrophic iron- and sulfur-oxidizing consortia shifting from Thiobacillus and Acidithiobacillus in early stages to Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans, Leptospirillum, and the acidophilic archaeon Ferroplasma (28-58% relative abundance in acidic/oxidized tailings). Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans and Ferroplasma acidiphilum express the outer-membrane monoheme c-type cytochrome Cyc2 (cyc2) and periplasmic Type-1 copper rusticyanins (AfR and FaR) alongside sulfur-oxidation, carbon-fixation, and heavy-metal detoxification pathways to catalyze extracellular electron transfer from ferrous iron (Fe2+) to regenerate ferric iron (Fe3+) mineral oxidant and drive tailings bioleaching and acid mine drainage.
