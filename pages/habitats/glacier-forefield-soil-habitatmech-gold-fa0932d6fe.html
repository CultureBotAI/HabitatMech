Glacier forefield deglaciation chronosequence nifH Cluster I/III diazotrophy, Methylocystis atmospheric C/N retrieval, and asynchronous C/N accumulation mechanism

Newly deglaciated glacier forefield soils experience extreme climatic fluctuations and severe carbon and nitrogen limitation across primary succession chronosequences. These oligotrophic mineral conditions select for diverse free-living and plant-symbiotic diazotrophs across Cyanobacteria, Alphaproteobacteria, Betaproteobacteria, and Firmicutes (including Nostoc, Geobacter, Polaromonas, Frankia, and methanotrophic Methylocystis spp. capable of retrieving both nitrogen and carbon from the atmospheric pool). These forefield consortia encode Cluster I and Cluster III nitrogenase (nifH), nitrite reductase (nirS), and carbon/sulfur/iron metabolic genes, catalyzing pioneer biological N2 fixation that builds labile nitrogen stocks, facilitates plant colonization, and drives asynchronous soil carbon and nitrogen accumulation along the deglaciation chronosequence.
