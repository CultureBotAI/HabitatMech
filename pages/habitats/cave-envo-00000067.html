Causal graphs

Subterranean cave aphotic oligotrophic and sulfidic/CO2 geochemical regime, Sulfurovum/Thiobacillus/Nitrosomonas/Pseudonocardia guild selection, and Sqr/SoxB/AmoA/RuBisCO (cbbL) dark chemolithoautotrophic primary production mechanism

Subterranean karst and sulfidic caves (such as Movile Cave, the Frasassi cave system, Zhijin Cave, and Luohandu Cave) are aphotic habitats isolated from photosynthetic energy input, characterized by oligotrophic carbonate rock walls and sediments, elevated cave-air CO2 (~2,000 ppm), and in sulfidic systems, H2S- and NH4+-bearing groundwater mixing with microoxic cave waters. Absence of light and availability of inorganic electron donors (H2S, S0, NH4+) and CO2 select for chemolithoautotrophic biofilms and sediment guilds including sulfide-oxidizing Epsilonproteobacteria (Sulfurovum-like and Sulfuricurvum spp.), Betaproteobacteria and Gammaproteobacteria sulfur oxidizers (Thiobacillus), ammonia and nitrite oxidizers (Nitrosomonas, Nitrospira, Candidatus Nitrotoga), and cbbL-bearing Actinobacteria (Pseudonocardia) and Alphaproteobacteria (Bradyrhizobium, Mesorhizobium). These communities express sulfide-quinone oxidoreductase (Sqr), thiosulfate-oxidizing Sox proteins (soxB), ammonia monooxygenase (amoA), nitrogenase (NifH), and form I ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (RuBisCO, cbbL) alongside 4-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydratase to drive dark inorganic carbon fixation, elemental sulfur S(0) and sulfate formation, and subterranean ecosystem primary production.
