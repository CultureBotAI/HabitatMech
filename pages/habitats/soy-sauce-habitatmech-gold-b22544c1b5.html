Causal graphs

Soy sauce high-salt koji/moromi brine and heptelidic acid gradient, Aspergillus oryzae / Tetragenococcus halophilus / Zygosaccharomyces rouxii succession, and protease/glutaminase/GAPDH/TCA umami fermentation mechanism

Brewed soy sauce fermentation progresses from solid-state soybean/wheat koji to a prolonged 120-day high-salt liquid moromi mash containing mold-derived secondary metabolites including the sesquiterpene lactone heptelidic acid (HA). This high-salt moromi environment selects for a halophilic microbial succession dominated by the koji mold Aspergillus oryzae (or A. sojae), the salt-tolerant lactic acid bacterium Tetragenococcus halophilus (alongside Weissella spp.), and the osmotolerant yeast Zygosaccharomyces rouxii. Aspergillus oryzae and Zygosaccharomyces rouxii secrete proteases, peptidases, glutaminases, aspartate aminotransferase, citrate synthase, aconitase, and isocitrate dehydrogenase to hydrolyze soybean proteins and synthesize umami flavor amino acids, while adapted Tetragenococcus halophilus strains carry resistance mutations in glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) that overcome A. oryzae heptelidic acid inhibition to sustain lactic acid fermentation under high-salt moromi conditions.
