Saline marsh Spartina alterniflora rhizosphere sulfidic redox gradient, Candidatus Thiodiazotropha and SRB/methanogen guild selection, and coupled oxidative-dsrAB/nifHDK/mcrA cycling mechanism

Tidal saline marshes vegetated by the foundation cordgrass Spartina alterniflora maintain steep rhizosphere and bulk-sediment redox gradients combining sulfidic, reduced sediment conditions with plant-derived organic substrates in the oxidized root zone. These gradients select for a core root and sediment microbiome dominated by sulfur-oxidizing symbionts of the genus Candidatus Thiodiazotropha alongside sulfate-reducing bacteria (Desulfobacteraceae and Desulfobulbaceae) and hydrogenotrophic/acetoclastic methanogens (Methanomicrobiales, Methanococcales, Methanosaeta). These guilds express oxidative and reductive dissimilatory sulfite reductase (oxidative-dsrAB, dsrA, dsrB), nitrogenase (nifHDK, nifK), ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase (RuBisCO), and methyl coenzyme M reductase A (mcrA) to couple chemolithoautotrophic sulfide oxidation with carbon and nitrogen fixation in stressed roots while driving sediment sulfate reduction and methanogenesis.
