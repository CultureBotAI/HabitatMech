Oil seep hydrocarbon and sulfate flux, ANME/Desulfosarcina-Desulfococcus and acidophilic alkane-degrader guild selection, and mcrA/dsrAB/alkB hydrocarbon oxidation mechanism

Marine and terrestrial hydrocarbon (oil) seeps discharge subsurface fluids enriched in methane, short-chain n-alkanes (ethane, propane, n-butane), and longer saturated acyclic and branched alkanes (C15-C30, pristane, phytane) into sulfate-bearing sediments and soils. These hydrocarbon and sulfate gradients select for anaerobic methane-oxidizing archaea (ANME-1 and ANME-2) and sulfate-reducing Deltaproteobacteria (Desulfosarcina/Desulfococcus clade including strain BuS5, Desulfobacteraceae, and Desulfobulbaceae) beneath Beggiatoa mats in marine seeps, as well as aerobic alkane-degrading heterotrophs (Acidisphaera and Acidiphilium) in acidic terrestrial seeps. These guilds express methyl coenzyme M reductase (mcrA), dissimilatory sulfite reductase (dsrAB), fumarate-activating alkylsuccinate pathway enzymes, and alkane monooxygenase (alkB) to catalyze anaerobic methane and short-chain alkane oxidation coupled to sulfate reduction alongside aerobic medium- and long-chain alkane mineralization.
