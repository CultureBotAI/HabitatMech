Causal graphs

Vaginal epithelial glycogen cleavage, Lactobacillus colonization, and lactic acid eubiosis mechanism

The lower female genital tract epithelium expresses glycogen that is cleaved by host mucosal alpha-amylase into maltose, maltotriose, and maltotetraose, supporting colonization by dominant Lactobacillus species (L. crispatus, L. iners, L. gasseri, L. jensenii) whose fermentation produces high concentrations of lactic acid (pH ~4.2-5.0) that maintain vaginal eubiosis and suppress bacterial vaginosis-associated strict anaerobes.
