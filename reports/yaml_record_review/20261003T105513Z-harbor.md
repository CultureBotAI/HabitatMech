landform, not the sheltered water itself. Both the
[current ENVO release JSON](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EnvironmentOntology/envo/master/envo.json)

retain `ENVO:01001884` surface landform; the local edge is at
`ontology_subclass_edges.tsv:5121`. Harbor is an accepted spelling synonym
of `ENVO:00000463` harbour, so spelling is not the defect.

The second parent, `ENVO:00001999` marine water body, comes from GOLD Marine.
These two parents combine sheltering-landform and water-body readings without
an item definition reconciling them. A water body sheltered by a landform is
not thereby a subtype of that landform. If the intended source is the
landform, the whole marine-water-body parent is instead unsupported.
This is a semantic scope conflict, not a claim of an independently tested
OWL disjointness axiom.

[NOAA's discussion of harbors of refuge](https://response.restoration.noaa.gov/node/1491)
illustrates the water-body reading: sheltering land and navigable water have
different roles. Its narrower safe-refuge example does not define every
GOLD harbor or prove that all source sites are navigable ports.
