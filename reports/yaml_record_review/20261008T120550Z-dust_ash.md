[pinned ENVO source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EnvironmentOntology/envo/a2455d1a77e46bb8a664d65a157166b539269042/envo.owl).
That source defines solid environmental material by physical state and ash by
combustion residue. Dust's definition does not exclude combustion-derived particles.

## Evidence

- The primary [BacDive isolation-source browser](https://bacdive.dsmz.de/isolation-sources)
  exposes distinct Dust and Dust (Ash) categories. More decisively,
  [strain 8468](https://bacdive.dsmz.de/strain/8468) explicitly carries
  Engineered / Waste / Dust (Ash), taxon 36813, and multiple house-dust isolation
  entries. This supports the ENGINEERED source classification and demonstrates
  that the bin is not exclusively combustion ash. It does not imply that every
  member came from a house.
- [Prakash and Lal 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16957112/), DOI
  10.1099/ijs.0.64080-0, supports a Sphingobium fuliginis type-strain isolation
  from a thermal-plant fly-ash dumping site. [Prakash et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17329778/),
  DOI 10.1099/ijs.0.64456-0, independently supports the analogous Pseudomonas
  delhiensis context. These are representative strain contexts, not proof that
  the entire historical BacDive category contains only two cleanly separable
  material sets, nor evidence of characteristic abundance.
- [Busse et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13130003/), DOI
  10.1099/ijs.0.02461-0, describes dust- and airborne isolates among the strains
  assigned to S. aurantiaca and S. faeni. It does not license a settled-only
  definition. Two browser fetches failed to expose article text; structured
  NCBI PubMed efetch subsequently returned and verified both those abstracts
  and their DOI/PMID pairs. No full-text sampling protocol audit was done.
