## Identity and Grounding

The exact GOLD path is `Environmental > Aquatic > Freshwater > Snow`,
minting `habitatmech:GOLD.483c00dc72`. Both actual default and applied
resolutions use `gold_leaf_label`, target `ENVO:01000406`, `EXACT`,
`skos:exactMatch`, no extra parent/xref and no reviewed decision. The
canonical snow label/definition and source leaf agree; the defective edge
does not require replacing that identity with a water-material identifier.

Typed ENVO asserts only `ENVO:00010483`, environmental material, as the
direct named superclass of snow. Its ice and other restrictions are not
additional named superclass assertions. `ENVO:00002011`, fresh water, is
instead added by `ingest_gold`'s second pass at `seed.py:898-907` from the
source parent path. The true ontology parent must remain.
[Snow](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ols4/api/ontologies/envo/terms?obo_id=ENVO%3A01000406),
[environmental material](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ols4/api/ontologies/envo/terms?obo_id=ENVO%3A00010483).

Fresh water is a low-solute subclass of `ENVO:00002006`, liquid water, whose
definition explicitly concerns liquid dihydrogen oxide. Primarily crystalline
ice flakes are not a subclass of that liquid material. Melting snow can
produce water; that process does not establish an is-a relationship.
[Fresh water](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ols4/api/ontologies/envo/terms?obo_id=ENVO%3A00002011),
[liquid water](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ols4/api/ontologies/envo/terms?obo_id=ENVO%3A00002006).

There is also no universal low-salinity restriction on the generic snow class.
The inspected Antarctic field study's section 3.1 reports saline snow over
pancake ice and discusses flooding and sampling/overwashing effects. This
is a bounded counterexample to treating all snow as freshwater material,
not evidence for the two GOLD organisms or a universal snow salinity value.
[Skatulla et al. 2022, DOI 10.5194/tc-16-2899-2022](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/16/2899/2022/tc-16-2899-2022.html).
